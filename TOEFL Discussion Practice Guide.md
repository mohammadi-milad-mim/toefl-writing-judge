# TOEFL Academic Discussion Practice Sheet

## 1. Main Goal

Write a clear, relevant, and well-developed contribution to the discussion.

Your response should:

- answer the professor’s question directly;
    
- clearly present your own position;
    
- agree briefly with one student’s useful point;
    
- add a different reason or perspective;
    
- explain that reason;
    
- support it with a specific example;
    
- address the opposing argument without naming the student;
    
- end with a clear and balanced conclusion.
    

### Recommended target

- **Length:** approximately 120–150 words
    
- **Format:** usually one cohesive paragraph
    
- **Time:** 10 minutes
    
- **Priority:** clear development, accurate language, and direct relevance
    

---

# 2. Core Structure

Use this seven-part structure:

## 1. State your position

Begin with:

> From my perspective, **[your position]**.

A fuller version is:

> From my perspective, **[your position]** would be the more effective approach.

## 2. Agree and add

Agree with one student and introduce your own different reason.

> I agree with **[Student A]** that **[valid point]**, and I would add that **[your new reason]**.

## 3. Explain your reason

> This is important because **[explanation]**.

## 4. Develop the reasoning

> When **[relevant situation]**, **[likely result]**.

## 5. Give a specific example

> For example, **[person or group + situation + action + result]**.

## 6. Address the opposing point

Do not name the second student.

> Although **[opposing concern]**, **[your response]**.

## 7. Conclude

> Therefore, **[balanced final conclusion]**.

---

# 3. Default Template

From my perspective, **[your position]** would be the more effective approach. I agree with **[Student A]** that **[one valid point]**, and I would add that **[your different main reason]**. This is important because **[explain why the reason matters]**. When **[relevant situation]**, **[explain the likely consequence]**. For example, **[specific person, situation, action, and result]**. Although **[state the opposing concern without naming the student]**, **[explain why it is limited, manageable, or less important]**. Therefore, **[balanced conclusion]**.

---

# 4. Faster Template

Use this version when the topic is difficult.

From my perspective, **[position]** would produce better results. I agree with **[Student A]** that **[valid point]**, and I would add that **[new contribution]**. This matters because **[explanation]**. For example, **[specific example and result]**. Although **[opposing concern]**, **[your response]**. Therefore, **[conclusion]**.

---

# 5. The Best Static Opening

## Recommended default

> From my perspective, **[position]**.

This phrase:

- clearly identifies the position as yours;
    
- sounds natural in an academic discussion;
    
- avoids repeating “I think” or “I believe”;
    
- works across almost every topic;
    
- is easy to use accurately under time pressure.
    

## Examples

> From my perspective, universities should continue offering recorded lectures.

> From my perspective, a hybrid working system would be the most effective approach.

> From my perspective, students should be allowed to use artificial intelligence under clear academic rules.

> From my perspective, requiring volunteer work would provide students with valuable practical experience.

## Slightly fuller version

> From my perspective, a flexible attendance policy would be more effective than a strict requirement.

## Other acceptable alternatives

Use these occasionally, but keep **“From my perspective”** as your main pattern.

- From my point of view, …
    
- The most effective approach would be to …
    
- A more practical solution would be to …
    
- The stronger option would be …
    
- A balanced policy would provide better results.
    

## Avoid

> My idea is that …

This is understandable but less natural in academic writing.

> I see it …

This is incomplete and too conversational.

> As far as I am concerned …

This is grammatically correct but often sounds unnecessarily formal or argumentative.

> Personally speaking, in my own personal view …

This is repetitive.

---

# 6. Agreement and Addition

The second sentence should agree with one student and then move forward with a new point.

## Default pattern

> I agree with Sara that **[valid point]**, and I would add that **[new reason]**.

## Other useful patterns

- I agree with Daniel’s point that **[valid idea]**, and another important consideration is **[new idea]**.
    
- Maya correctly emphasizes **[valid point]**, and this approach could also **[new benefit]**.
    
- Building on Alex’s point, **[your additional argument]**.
    
- Sara raises an important issue, and a further advantage is **[your new reason]**.
    
- Daniel’s argument about **[topic]** is convincing, and this policy could additionally **[new benefit]**.
    
- The benefit that Maya identifies is important, and another benefit is **[new contribution]**.
    

## Weak version

> I agree with Sara.

This contributes nothing new.

## Better version

> I agree with Sara that internships provide practical experience, and I would add that they help students determine whether a particular career suits them.

---

# 7. Add a Different Reason

Do not merely restate Student A’s argument.

## Student A says

> Volunteer work teaches empathy.

## Repetition

> I agree because volunteering helps students understand other people.

## New contribution

> I agree with Maria that volunteering develops empathy, and I would add that it prepares students for professional responsibilities.

Possible categories for your new reason:

- practical skills;
    
- academic performance;
    
- employment;
    
- responsibility;
    
- independence;
    
- communication;
    
- accessibility;
    
- cost;
    
- time;
    
- fairness;
    
- motivation;
    
- health;
    
- social interaction;
    
- environmental impact;
    
- long-term consequences.
    

---

# 8. Explain the Reason

After introducing your reason, explain exactly how it supports your position.

## Useful phrases

- This is important because …
    
- This matters because …
    
- The main advantage is that …
    
- This would allow students to …
    
- This can help people …
    
- One likely result is …
    
- Without this support, …
    
- This is especially valuable for …
    
- When people have access to …, they can …
    
- This benefit is difficult to obtain through …
    
- As a result, …
    

## Weak development

> Internships are useful because students gain experience.

## Strong development

> Internships allow students to apply classroom knowledge in real working environments. They also reveal which professional skills students still need to develop.

---

# 9. Develop One Reason Deeply

One developed reason is usually stronger than several undeveloped reasons.

## Weak

> Online learning is cheaper, easier, faster, modern, and flexible.

## Better

> The main advantage of online learning is accessibility. Students who live far from campus can attend lectures without spending several hours commuting each day.

Use this progression:

> **Reason → explanation → situation → result**

Example:

> Recorded lectures improve understanding because students can review difficult explanations. When a topic is technically complex, hearing the explanation a second time may help students identify details they missed during class.

---

# 10. The Example Formula

A useful example contains:

> **Person or group → situation → action → result**

## Weak example

> For example, online classes are useful.

## Strong example

> For example, a student who works in the evening could watch a recorded lecture earlier in the day instead of missing the course entirely.

## Example starters

- For example, a student who …
    
- Consider a situation in which …
    
- A university student working part-time may …
    
- A school could, for instance, …
    
- Suppose that a student …
    
- This can be seen when …
    
- In my own experience, …
    

## Example checklist

Your example should answer:

- Who is involved?
    
- What situation are they facing?
    
- What do they do?
    
- What result follows?
    

---

# 11. Address the Opposing Point Naturally

Do not write:

> Although Andrew is right that students are busy, …

Instead, address the concern directly:

> Although students already have demanding schedules, schools could offer volunteer opportunities during weekends or vacations.

This sounds more natural because the focus remains on the issue rather than the student.

---

# 12. Counterargument Patterns

## A. Solve the concern

> Although the program may be expensive, universities could begin with a small pilot project.

## B. Limit the concern

> Although the policy may create difficulties for some students, the problem would not affect every participant equally.

## C. Show that the benefit is greater

> Although the policy may create short-term inconvenience, its long-term educational benefits would be more significant.

## D. Add a condition

> Although students may misuse artificial intelligence, this risk could be reduced through clear rules and source disclosure.

## E. Offer a compromise

> Although online learning may reduce personal interaction, universities could maintain weekly in-person seminars.

## F. Challenge the assumption

> Although this concern is understandable, it assumes that all students learn effectively in the same environment.

## G. Reduce the scale of the problem

> Although the requirement could increase students’ workloads, limiting it to a few hours each month would prevent it from becoming excessive.

---

# 13. Final-Sentence Patterns

The final sentence should combine your position with your response to the opposing concern.

- Therefore, a flexible requirement would provide valuable experience without placing an excessive burden on students.
    
- Therefore, a hybrid system would maintain flexibility while providing regular academic support.
    
- Therefore, regulated AI use would be more effective than either unrestricted use or a complete ban.
    
- Therefore, this policy should be introduced gradually and supported by clear guidelines.
    
- Therefore, the proposed approach would preserve the main benefits while reducing the most serious disadvantage.
    
- Therefore, a balanced solution would meet students’ needs more effectively than either extreme.
    

Avoid:

> In conclusion, this is my opinion.

# 14. Model Response 1: Volunteer Work

## Question

Should high schools require students to complete volunteer work?

- Maria: Volunteering teaches empathy.
    
- Andrew: Students are already too busy.
    

## Model answer

From my perspective, a reasonable volunteer-work requirement would benefit high school students. I agree with Maria that volunteering can make students more aware of other people’s needs, and I would add that it prepares them for future professional responsibilities. Volunteer activities require students to manage their time, communicate with unfamiliar people, and complete assigned tasks. For example, a student helping at a community event may need to coordinate with other volunteers, assist visitors, and solve unexpected problems. These experiences can develop practical skills that ordinary classes do not always provide. Although students already have demanding schedules, schools could offer flexible opportunities during weekends or vacations. Therefore, a limited and flexible requirement would provide meaningful experience without creating an excessive burden.

## Structure

- **Position:** A reasonable requirement is beneficial.
    
- **Agreement:** Volunteering develops empathy.
    
- **New reason:** It prepares students for employment.
    
- **Explanation:** It develops responsibility and communication.
    
- **Example:** Helping at a community event.
    
- **Opposing point:** Students are busy.
    
- **Response:** Offer flexible scheduling.
    
- **Conclusion:** Use a limited requirement.

# 15. Model Response 2: In-Person Classes

## Question

Should first-year university students attend classes in person?

- Lena: In-person classes help students form relationships.
    
- Omar: Online classes provide flexibility.
    

## Model answer

From my perspective, a mixed system would be the most effective approach for first-year students. I agree with Lena that in-person classes help new students form relationships, and I would add that regular classroom contact makes academic support easier to access. First-year students may not recognize their difficulties early enough to request help independently. For example, during a weekly seminar, an instructor may notice that a student is confused, frequently absent, or struggling with assignments. The instructor can then provide guidance before the problem becomes more serious. Although online lectures offer greater flexibility, universities could keep large lectures online while requiring smaller discussion classes in person. Therefore, a hybrid system would provide both academic support and control over students’ schedules.

---

# 16. Model Response 3: Group Projects

## Question

Should university courses include more group projects?

- Nina: Group projects improve teamwork.
    
- Robert: Some students do not contribute equally.
    

## Model answer

From my perspective, carefully designed group projects would improve university courses. I agree with Nina that these assignments develop teamwork skills, and I would add that they prepare students for professional environments. In many jobs, employees must divide responsibilities, communicate progress, and combine different forms of expertise. For example, a software project may require programmers, designers, and project managers to coordinate their work closely. Completing similar assignments at university can help students practice these responsibilities before entering the workplace. Although some students may contribute less than others, instructors can reduce this problem through individual evaluations and peer feedback. Therefore, group projects can provide valuable preparation while still rewarding individual effort fairly.

---

# 17. Model Response 4: Recorded Lectures

## Question

Should professors record all university lectures?

- David: Recordings help absent students.
    
- Emma: Students may stop attending class.
    

## Model answer

From my perspective, making most lectures available as recordings would improve students’ access to course material. I agree with David that recordings help students who miss class, and I would add that they are valuable tools for reviewing difficult subjects. Students often need to hear a complex explanation more than once before fully understanding it. For example, an engineering student preparing for an examination could replay the part of a lecture that explains a difficult formula instead of relying only on incomplete notes. Although some students may attend class less frequently, professors could still require participation in discussions, laboratories, or short quizzes. Therefore, recorded lectures can support learning without removing the benefits of regular participation.

---

# 18. Model Response 5: Artificial Intelligence

## Question

Should students be allowed to use artificial intelligence tools for assignments?

- Sara: AI can help students generate ideas.
    
- Daniel: Students may become dependent on it.
    

## Model answer

From my perspective, allowing limited and transparent use of artificial intelligence would be more effective than banning it completely. I agree with Sara that AI can help students generate ideas, and I would add that it can provide immediate explanations when instructors are unavailable. For example, a student who does not understand a statistical concept could ask an AI tool for a simpler explanation and then verify it using course materials. This use supports learning rather than replacing the student’s work. Although students may become overly dependent on technology, schools could require them to disclose how they used AI and explain their final reasoning independently. Therefore, clear rules would preserve the educational benefits of AI while reducing the risk of misuse.

---

# 19. Model Response 6: Remote Work

## Question

Should companies allow employees to work from home?

- Maya: Remote work saves commuting time.
    
- James: Employees collaborate better in an office.
    

## Model answer

From my perspective, a hybrid working system would produce the best results. I agree with Maya that remote work saves commuting time, and I would add that it can help employees concentrate on tasks that require uninterrupted attention. At home, workers may be able to complete reports, programming tasks, or data analysis without frequent office interruptions. For example, a software developer could spend two days working remotely on a complex feature and attend the office on other days for meetings and team planning. Although face-to-face communication can improve collaboration, companies can preserve this benefit by scheduling regular in-person workdays. Therefore, a hybrid policy would support both individual productivity and effective teamwork.

# 20. Flexible Variations

The default structure is useful, but do not force it when the question requires something different.

## A. Agree with one student

> Position → agreement → new reason → explanation → example → opposing concern → response

## B. Agree with both students

> From my perspective, both points are valid, and a combined policy would address both concerns.

Then explain the combined solution.

## C. Disagree with both students

> From my perspective, neither position fully addresses the main issue, which is **[your new criterion]**.

Then develop your alternative.

## D. Offer a compromise

> From my perspective, a hybrid system would be more practical than choosing either option completely.

## E. Extend one idea

> I agree with Sara that online courses provide flexibility, and I would add that they improve access for students who live far from campus.

# 27. Safe Sentence Structures

## Cause

> Recorded lectures are useful because students can review difficult explanations.

## Result

> Students can review difficult explanations, so recordings may improve understanding.

## Addition

> Group projects develop teamwork, and they also prepare students for professional collaboration.

## Contrast

> Online lectures offer flexibility, whereas in-person seminars provide direct interaction.

## Concession

> Although online courses are convenient, first-year students may need regular face-to-face support.

## Condition

> If universities provide both formats, students can choose the option that best suits their needs.

## Relative clause

> Students who work part-time may benefit from flexible schedules.

# 28. Common Weak Patterns

## Weak opening

> I think maybe universities should consider online courses.

## Better

> From my perspective, online courses would provide universities with a more flexible teaching option.

---

## Weak agreement

> I agree with Sara because she is correct.

## Better

> I agree with Sara that internships provide practical experience, and I would add that they help students explore possible careers.

---

## Unnecessary contrast during agreement

> I agree with Sara that volunteering teaches empathy, but it also teaches responsibility.

## Better

> I agree with Sara that volunteering teaches empathy, and I would add that it teaches responsibility.

---

## Naming the student in the counterargument

> Although Robert is right that some students contribute less, peer evaluation can solve the problem.

## Better

> Although some students may contribute less, peer evaluation and individual grading can reduce this problem.

---

## Listing reasons

> It is cheaper, easier, faster, modern, and useful.

## Better

> Its main advantage is accessibility. Students who live far from campus can attend lectures without spending several hours commuting.

---

## Vague example

> For example, this happens to many students.

## Better

> For example, a student with an evening job could watch a recorded lecture earlier in the day instead of missing the course.

---

## Empty conclusion

> In conclusion, this is the best idea.

## Better

> Therefore, a hybrid system would maintain flexibility while providing regular academic support.

# 29. Ten-Minute Test Routine

## 0:00–1:00 — Plan

Identify:

- your position;
    
- Student A’s useful point;
    
- your new reason;
    
- the explanation;
    
- the example;
    
- the opposing concern;
    
- your response.
    

## 1:00–8:30 — Write

Follow:

> Perspective → agreement and addition → explanation → example → opposing concern → response → conclusion

## 8:30–10:00 — Review

Check:

1. Did I begin with “From my perspective”?
    
2. Is my position immediately clear?
    
3. Did I agree and add rather than agree and repeat?
    
4. Did I explain why my reason matters?
    
5. Is my example specific?
    
6. Did I address the opposing point without naming the student?
    
7. Did I solve, limit, or outweigh the concern?
    
8. Are my sentences complete and accurate?
    

---

# 30. Final Self-Check

## Position

- Did I answer the professor’s exact question?
    
- Did I clearly present my view?
    
- Is my opening direct and natural?
    

## Agreement

- Did I identify one useful point from Student A?
    
- Did I use an additive structure?
    
- Is my reason different from Student A’s reason?
    

## Development

- Did I explain how or why my reason matters?
    
- Did I provide a specific example?
    
- Does the example clearly support my argument?
    

## Opposing argument

- Did I address the point rather than the person?
    
- Did I avoid naming Student B?
    
- Did I solve, limit, qualify, or outweigh the concern?
    

## Language

- Does every sentence have a subject and verb?
    
- Are verb forms and plural nouns correct?
    
- Did I avoid unnecessary repetition?
    
- Are there obvious spelling or punctuation mistakes?
    

---

# 31. Final Structure to Memorize

> From my perspective, **[position]** would be the more effective approach.  
> I agree with **[Student A]** that **[valid point]**, and I would add that **[new reason]**.  
> This matters because **[explanation]**.  
> For example, **[specific example and result]**.  
> Although **[opposing concern]**, **[response]**.  
> Therefore, **[balanced conclusion]**.

## Argumentative Spine

> From my perspective, **X** is the stronger position.  
> Student A identifies one benefit, and **Y** is another important reason.  
> **Y** matters because of **Z**.  
> The example demonstrates **Z**.  
> The opposing concern can be solved, limited, or outweighed.  
> Therefore, **X** remains the more effective approach.
