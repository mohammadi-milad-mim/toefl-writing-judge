## How to Use This Guide

Do not memorize complete emails. Memorize:

1. one core structure for each formality level;
    
2. several purpose-specific opening and closing sentences;
    
3. the body structure for each common task;
    
4. phrases for explaining, requesting, suggesting, and developing ideas.
    

A strong response will usually be around **100–130 words**, although completeness and clarity matter more than an exact word count.

---

# 1. Core Email Structures by Formality

## A. Formal Core Template

Use for professors, supervisors, managers, administrators, and unfamiliar professionals.

> Dear [Title + Last Name],
> 
> I hope you are doing well. I am writing to [state the exact purpose].
> 
> [Address the first prompt requirement and add a relevant detail.]  
> [Address the second requirement and explain its reason, effect, or importance.]  
> [Address the third requirement and state the requested action, recommendation, or next step.]
> 
> Thank you for your time and consideration. Please let me know if you need any additional information. I look forward to your response.
> 
> Best regards,  
> Milad

### Formal language

- I am writing to request...
    
- I am writing regarding...
    
- I am writing to inquire about...
    
- Would it be possible to...?
    
- I would appreciate it if you could...
    
- I would be grateful if you could...
    

---

## B. Semi-Formal Core Template

Use for club coordinators, teaching assistants, colleagues, event organizers, familiar university staff, and classmates you do not know closely.

> Hello [Name],
> 
> I hope you are doing well. I am writing about [state the topic or purpose].
> 
> [Address the first prompt requirement and add a useful detail.]  
> [Address the second requirement and explain the situation or consequence.]  
> [Address the third requirement and suggest or request the next action.]
> 
> Thank you for your help. Please let me know what you think or if you need any additional information.
> 
> Best regards,  
> Milad

### Semi-formal language

- I wanted to contact you about...
    
- I am writing about...
    
- I wanted to ask whether...
    
- Could you please...?
    
- Could we consider...?
    
- Please let me know whether...
    

---

## C. Informal Core Template

Use for friends, close classmates, roommates, and people you know personally.

> Hi [First Name],
> 
> I hope you’re doing well. I wanted to [ask/tell/invite/thank] you about [topic].
> 
> [Explain the main situation naturally.]  
> [Add the relevant reason, effect, or personal detail.]  
> [State what you would like the person to do or suggest the next step.]
> 
> Thanks again. Let me know what works best for you, and feel free to call me if you want to discuss it.
> 
> Best,  
> Milad

### Informal language

- I wanted to ask you about...
    
- I wanted to let you know that...
    
- Could you...?
    
- Can you let me know...?
    
- Let me know what works for you.
    
- Feel free to call me.

# 2. Choosing the Correct Formality

|Element|Formal|Semi-formal|Informal|
|---|---|---|---|
|Recipient|Professor, manager, administrator|Coordinator, colleague, teaching assistant|Friend, roommate, close classmate|
|Greeting|Dear Professor Lee,|Hello Ms. Patel,|Hi Daniel,|
|Warm opening|I hope you are doing well.|I hope you are doing well.|I hope you’re doing well.|
|Purpose|I am writing to request...|I am writing about...|I wanted to ask you about...|
|Request|Would it be possible to...?|Could we consider...?|Could you...?|
|Closing|Thank you for your time and consideration.|Thank you for your help.|Thanks again.|
|Sign-off|Best regards,|Best regards, / Best,|Best, / Take care,|

## Safest default

When the relationship is unclear, use a polite semi-formal-to-formal style:

> Dear [Title/Name],
> 
> I hope you are doing well. I am writing to [purpose].
> 
> Thank you for your time and consideration. Please let me know if you need any additional information.
> 
> Best regards,  
> Milad

Avoid being too informal when uncertain.

# 3. Opening System

A reliable opening contains two parts:

> **Warm sentence + direct purpose**

For example:

> I hope you are doing well. I am writing to request a short extension for the assignment.

“I hope you are doing well” can be used across formal, semi-formal, and informal emails. In informal emails, use the contracted form:

> I hope you’re doing well.

“I hope this email finds you well” is also correct, but “I hope you are doing well” is simpler and easier to write accurately.

# 4. Building the Body

The body should address every prompt requirement. Most TOEFL prompts contain several communicative functions, such as:

> appreciation → problem → request

or:

> apology → explanation → solution

Give each important requirement one or two sentences.

## Development patterns

### Fact → consequence

> The room change was announced only thirty minutes before the event. As a result, several students waited in the wrong room.

### Problem → example

> The registration form did not work properly. For example, it did not accept international telephone numbers.

### Request → reason

> Could you send the reading list before Friday? This would give students enough time to obtain the materials.

### Suggestion → benefit

> I suggest sending schedule changes by email. This would ensure that all participants receive the information quickly.

### Apology → corrective action

> I apologize for submitting the report late. I will send the completed version by tomorrow afternoon.

### Appreciation → impact

> I especially appreciated your comments on my presentation because they helped me explain the main result more clearly.

---

# 5. Adding Useful Length

Do not make the email longer through repetition. Add length by developing the message.

## Safe ways to add one useful sentence

### Explain the consequence

> As a result, several participants missed the beginning of the workshop.

### Explain why the request matters

> This would allow me to complete the assignment more accurately.

### Give a specific example

> For example, two students traveled to the original location and could not reach the new room on time.

### Explain the expected benefit

> This change would reduce confusion and improve communication.

### Offer an alternative

> If Monday is not possible, I would be happy to submit the assignment on Sunday evening.

### Offer help

> I would be happy to help update the contact list for the next event.

### Offer further information

> Please let me know if you need any additional information.

### Offer a discussion

> I would be happy to discuss this further at your convenience.

Use a call or meeting only when further discussion is relevant. For most tasks, the safest additional sentence is:

> Please let me know if you need any additional information.

# 6. Closing System

A strong closing usually contains:

> **appreciation + next step**

For example:

> Thank you for considering my request. I look forward to your response.

# 7. Purpose-Specific Email Modules

Insert the appropriate body module into one of the three core formality templates.

---

## A. Request or Permission

### Structure

Purpose → background → reason → specific request → alternative

### Body module

> [Explain the relevant situation.] Unfortunately, [circumstance], which means that [consequence]. Would it be possible to [specific request]? If necessary, I would be happy to [alternative or compensating action].

### Useful phrases

- Would it be possible to...?
    
- Could you please...?
    
- I would appreciate it if you could...
    
- I would be grateful if you could...
    
- May I attend the alternative session?
    
- Please let me know whether this is possible.
    

### Example

> Unfortunately, the university website was unavailable yesterday, so I could not upload the completed assignment. Would it be possible to submit it by Monday morning? I would be happy to email you the current version today.

---

## B. Reporting a Problem or Complaint

### Structure

Exact problem → consequence → requested solution → prevention

### Body module

> When I [action], [explain what happened]. As a result, [describe the consequence]. Could you please [specific solution]? To prevent similar problems in the future, it may also be helpful to [recommendation].

### Useful phrases

- Unfortunately,...
    
- The main issue was that...
    
- I was unable to...
    
- As a result,...
    
- This caused considerable confusion.
    
- This resulted in a delay.
    
- One possible solution would be to...
    

### Tone

Avoid:

> You failed to inform us.

Use:

> Unfortunately, several participants did not receive the updated information.

---

## C. Scheduling or Rescheduling

### Structure

Reason → proposed times → flexibility → confirmation

### Body module

> Unfortunately, [brief reason], so I will not be available at the original time. I am available on [option one] or [option two]. However, I would be happy to adjust my schedule if another time is more convenient for you.

### Useful phrases

- arrange a meeting;
    
- schedule an appointment;
    
- reschedule our discussion;
    
- postpone the meeting;
    
- confirm the time;
    
- at your convenience;
    
- a suitable time;
    
- whichever time works best.
    

### Closing

> Please let me know which option works best. Thank you for your understanding.

---

## D. Invitation

### Structure

Event details → purpose → reason for inviting → response request

### Body module

> The event will take place on [date] at [time/location]. Its purpose is to [purpose]. We would be especially pleased if you could [attend/speak/participate] because [specific reason].

### Useful phrases

- We would be delighted if you could join us.
    
- The event will take place on...
    
- Your experience would provide a valuable perspective.
    
- Your participation would be greatly appreciated.
    
- We hope to see you there.
    

### Closing

> Please let me know whether you are available to attend. I hope you will be able to join us.

---

## E. Apology

### Structure

Direct apology → brief explanation → recognition of impact → corrective action → prevention

### Body module

> Unfortunately, [brief explanation]. I understand that this [caused inconvenience/delayed the work/affected the group]. To correct the situation, I will [specific action]. I will also [preventive action] to ensure that the same problem does not happen again.

### Useful phrases

- I sincerely apologize for...
    
- I am sorry that I was unable to...
    
- I understand the inconvenience this caused.
    
- I take responsibility for...
    
- To correct the situation,...
    
- I will make sure this does not happen again.
    

Do not spend the entire email defending yourself.

---

## F. Recommendation or Suggestion

### Structure

Recommendation → reason → implementation → expected benefit

### Body module

> I recommend [specific proposal] because [reason]. For example, [explain how it could be implemented]. This would help [expected benefit] and reduce [current problem].

### Useful phrases

- I would recommend...
    
- One possible improvement would be to...
    
- It may be helpful to...
    
- The organization could consider...
    
- This would make the process more efficient.
    
- This would help ensure that...
    
- To prevent similar problems in the future,...
    

---

## G. Follow-Up or Status Inquiry

### Structure

Previous action → relevant detail → status question → reason

### Body module

> I sent [submission/application/request] on [date], but I have not yet received confirmation. I wanted to make sure that it was received successfully. Could you please let me know its current status? I would appreciate an update because [deadline or reason].

### Useful phrases

- I am following up on...
    
- I wanted to check whether...
    
- Could you please confirm that...?
    
- Could you provide an update?
    
- I have not yet received confirmation.
    
- I would appreciate any information you can provide.
    

Avoid:

> Why have you not answered me?

Use:

> I understand that you may be busy, but I would appreciate any update you can provide.

---

## H. Thank-You Email

### Structure

Specific thanks → important detail → impact → future use

### Body module

> I especially appreciated [specific detail] because it helped me [benefit or result]. Your support made it much easier to [specific outcome], and I will make sure to apply your advice when I [future action].

### Useful phrases

- Thank you very much for...
    
- I truly appreciate...
    
- I was especially grateful for...
    
- Your advice was extremely helpful.
    
- Your support allowed me to...
    
- I learned a great deal from...
    

Avoid vague appreciation:

> Thank you for everything. It was very good.

Use specific appreciation:

> Thank you for reviewing my presentation. Your suggestion to simplify the final slide made the conclusion much clearer.

---

## I. Accepting an Invitation

### Structure

Thanks → clear acceptance → confirmation → preparation question

### Body module

> Thank you for inviting me to [event]. I would be delighted to attend and can confirm that I am available on [date/time]. Please let me know if there is anything I should prepare or bring.

### Useful phrases

- I would be delighted to attend.
    
- I am pleased to accept your invitation.
    
- I can confirm that I will be available.
    
- Please let me know if any preparation is required.
    

---

## J. Politely Declining

### Structure

Thanks → clear refusal → brief reason → alternative or goodwill

### Body module

> Thank you very much for inviting me to [event]. Unfortunately, I will not be able to attend because [brief reason]. I am sorry to miss the event. If possible, I would be happy to [alternative action or attend another time].

### Useful phrases

- Unfortunately, I will not be able to...
    
- I am afraid I cannot attend...
    
- I appreciate the invitation; however,...
    
- I would be happy to participate on another occasion.
    
- I hope we can arrange another time.
    

### Closing

> Thank you for your understanding. I hope the event goes very well.

---

## K. Giving Information or an Update

### Structure

New information → reason → consequence → required action

### Body module

> Due to [reason], [explain what has changed]. This means that [consequence]. Please [required action], and let me know if you have any questions.

### Useful phrases

- I wanted to update you about...
    
- Please note that...
    
- There has been a change to...
    
- Due to unforeseen circumstances,...
    
- As a result,...
    
- Please confirm that you received this information.

# 8. Combining Different Functions

Many prompts combine several email purposes. Do not force the whole prompt into only one category.

## Example functional plan

Suppose the prompt asks you to:

1. say what you liked;
    
2. describe a submission problem;
    
3. ask whether your work was received.
    

Your plan should be:

> appreciation → problem → follow-up request

## Combined structure

> I especially appreciate [specific positive feature]. Unfortunately, when I [action], [problem occurred]. As a result, I am uncertain whether [item] was received. Could you please confirm its current status? If necessary, I would be happy to submit it again.

During planning, label each prompt requirement with its function.

# 13. Seven-Minute Writing Method

## First 40 seconds: analyze

Identify:

- recipient;
    
- correct formality;
    
- main purpose;
    
- requirement 1;
    
- requirement 2;
    
- requirement 3;
    
- requested next action.
    

Create a short functional plan:

> appreciation → problem → request

or:

> apology → explanation → corrective action

---

## Next five minutes: write

Write:

1. greeting;
    
2. warm opening;
    
3. exact purpose;
    
4. one or two sentences for each requirement;
    
5. relevant closing;
    
6. sign-off.
    

Do not stop repeatedly to correct every word while drafting.

---

## Final minute: check

Check in this order:

1. Did I answer every prompt requirement?
    
2. Is my purpose immediately clear?
    
3. Is my request or next step specific?
    
4. Did I add at least one explanation, consequence, example, or benefit?
    
5. Is the tone appropriate for the recipient?
    
6. Are all sentences complete?
    
7. Are verbs, plurals, and articles correct?
    
8. Are common words spelled correctly?
    
9. Is the closing relevant?
